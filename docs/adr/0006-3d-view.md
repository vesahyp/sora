## Context

Since ADR 0005 every car is a 3D Rapier body on four raycast wheels:
it pitches under braking, rolls in a bend, and each wheel rides its
spring. None of it could be seen. The game drew a flat top view
(ADR 0001), and the ground was level everywhere but the river banks
and one crest per track, so the springs barely moved. Vesa, after the
tuning panel shipped: "Its not really 3d so i cant even see how the
suspension or tyres or anything works. Plus its flat so suspension is
useless."

## Decision

The race is drawn in 3D, in three slices, each shipped and checked on
the phone:

1. **A 3D view of today's world** (`src/render/render3d.ts`), three.js
   over the same Rapier world. The ground is the track's own heights
   (`Track.groundAt`); the roadside is the top view's scenery placed in
   3D (trees, bales, the crowd, the power line, the barn). Each car is
   drawn where the physics has it: the body at its pitch and roll, each
   wheel at its spring's length below its mount, the front pair steered,
   all four spinning (`carPose` in physics.ts). The camera is behind
   the car, its direction the car's travel blended a third of the way
   toward the road 16 m ahead, so a bend shows before the car turns
   in. The top view stays as an option (title screen: Camera), and
   draws the minimap over the 3D view. The scene is mirrored in y, so a
   right swing of the thumb is a right turn in both views.
2. **Real terrain under the physics.** `groundAt(s, d)` becomes a
   height across the road as well as along it: hills along the lap,
   bends banked toward their inside, the crests and the rivers as now,
   and on gravel small bumps and two worn ruts where the wheels run.
   The physics' ground mesh gets the resolution to carry them, the
   checks (`sim-check`'s flights, `physics-check`'s crest and river)
   are re-read, and the tracks' heights are data in `tracks.ts`.
3. **Readability and speed on the phone**: the chase camera's height
   and lead tuned by thumb, dust and the weapons' effects in 3D, and a
   frame time read off the playthrough on the iPhone layout.

## Consequences

- three.js adds about 150 kB gzipped to the download.
- The phone scripts launch Chromium on the Mac's GPU
  (`scripts/gpu.mjs`): in software WebGL the race ran at 3 frames a
  second and every script's clock ran out.
- The top view's sprites (`sprites.ts`, the lineup, `make car-shots`)
  stay for the top view; the chase view's cars are simple boxes in the
  car's colours until slice 3 shapes them by body.
- The HUD's steering-wheel icon is hidden in the chase view: it sat
  where the car is.
- A frame's work moves to the GPU. Measured in slice 3 (`make
  frame-check`, the iPhone landscape layout): 60 frames a second at
  full and at half CPU speed, the game's own work 2.3 ms a frame at full
  speed; at a quarter, an old phone, 55 a second. The forest casts no
  shadow, to keep the shadow pass to the cars and the roadside.
- Slice 3 also gave each body its own proportions in the chase view
  (`SHAPES`: the van and the bus tall, the tractor's big rear wheels
  and stack, the plough's blade, the pickup's bed), dust from the wheels
  on gravel and grass, and a camera that pulls back, rises and opens
  its lens with speed.
